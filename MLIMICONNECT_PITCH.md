# MlimiConnect Pitch

## One-line pitch

MlimiConnect is a mobile-first agricultural commerce and advisory platform that helps farmers sell produce and equipment, helps buyers source trusted products, and connects the whole transaction to payments, logistics, traceability, expert advice, and market intelligence.

## 60-second pitch

Agricultural trade in Malawi is still fragmented. Farmers often struggle to find reliable buyers, understand fair market prices, access timely advice, arrange transport, and receive secure payment. Buyers and institutions face the opposite problem: finding verified suppliers, consistent quantities, transparent product history, and dependable fulfilment.

MlimiConnect brings these activities into one mobile-first platform. Farmers and equipment sellers can publish listings, receive orders, communicate with buyers, monitor payments, and access AI or human agricultural advice. Individuals, companies, cooperatives, NGOs, government bodies, and institutions can discover produce, farm inputs, tools, and equipment; place orders; track supply; and verify traceability records.

Our business model combines a 3.5% commission on settled transactions with optional subscriptions and promoted listings. The platform is designed for smartphones, supports English and Chichewa, includes a low-data experience and PWA installation, and has a USSD path for users without reliable smartphone access.

MlimiConnect is not simply another online shop. It is digital infrastructure for agricultural trade—from discovery and advice to fulfilment and measurable impact.

## The problem

- Farmers have limited access to buyers and can be forced to accept poor prices.
- Buyers struggle to identify trusted suppliers with sufficient stock and reliable delivery.
- Produce, farm inputs, tools, and equipment are traded through disconnected channels.
- Market-price information and agricultural advice do not always reach farmers at the right time.
- Payments, transport, communication, disputes, and traceability are rarely managed in one place.
- Many digital products overlook low-bandwidth users and local-language accessibility.

## The solution

MlimiConnect provides one connected workflow:

1. A farmer, cooperative, company, or independent seller lists produce, inputs, tools, or equipment.
2. Buyers search by category, location, price, availability, and seller information.
3. Buyers and sellers communicate through in-platform messaging and configurable alerts.
4. Orders, delivery status, disputes, reviews, and wallet activity are recorded through the platform.
5. Traceability batches provide a verifiable history for products where provenance matters.
6. Farmers access market information plus AI-assisted or expert agricultural advisory services.
7. Administrators monitor marketplace health, users, orders, revenue, and platform settings through analytics dashboards.

## Business model

### Transaction revenue

A 3.5% platform commission is recognized only after a successful settled order. Payment-processing costs should be shown separately and transparently.

### Subscriptions

- Farmer Plus: enhanced visibility, analytics, advisory allowances, and seller tools
- Buyer Pro: sourcing tools, analytics, alerts, and purchasing support
- Organisation or Institution plans: team access, reporting, procurement, and programme-level capabilities

### Additional revenue

- Clearly labelled promoted listings
- Expert consultation fees
- Logistics or fulfilment referral revenue through approved partners
- Institutional analytics and programme reporting
- Traceability and verification services for commercial supply chains

## Go-to-market strategy

1. Pilot in one focused agricultural corridor with a small number of crops and verified farmer groups.
2. Recruit anchor buyers such as processors, retailers, restaurants, schools, and institutional procurement programmes.
3. Onboard farmers through cooperatives, extension workers, NGOs, and demonstration days.
4. Integrate trusted mobile-money and transport partners after validating the order workflow.
5. Publish verified transaction and impact results, then expand geographically and by product category.

The first pilot should prioritise marketplace liquidity: enough credible sellers and committed buyers in the same location at the same time.

1. Start on the mobile landing page and explain the value proposition.
2. Sign in as a farmer and create or show a produce/equipment listing.
3. Switch to a buyer account, search for that category, and demonstrate the buying flow.

Keep the demo focused on one story—for example, a maize farmer supplying a processor—rather than opening every screen.

## Likely questions and strong answers

### Is this just eBay for agriculture?

No. The marketplace is one component. MlimiConnect also addresses agricultural advisory, local market information, transaction communication, role-based institutional participation, fulfilment, and product traceability. It is designed around agricultural workflows and local access conditions.

### How will you attract both farmers and buyers?

Begin with anchor buyers who have recurring demand, then onboard organised farmer groups capable of supplying those products. Concentrating on selected crops and locations avoids spreading early liquidity too thinly.

### How do you include users without modern smartphones?

The main product is mobile-first, installable as a PWA, bilingual, and designed to support low-data use. A companion USSD service provides a path for basic-phone access, while assisted onboarding through cooperatives and extension workers can cover more complex tasks.

### How will payments be protected?

The product models checkout, wallet, order status, disputes, and settlement. Production launch requires integration with licensed payment providers and clearly defined settlement, refund, identity-verification, and dispute policies. Never describe escrow as live until the legal and provider arrangements are complete.

### What prevents fraud or low-quality listings?

Role verification, listing moderation, transaction records, reviews, traceability, reporting, and administrator oversight create layers of trust. Higher-risk categories can require stronger verification before publication or payment release.

### Where does the AI advice come from?

AI is an assistance layer, not a replacement for agricultural professionals. Advice should use approved sources, clearly state limitations, preserve user privacy, and escalate high-risk cases to qualified experts.

### How will the platform make money without excluding farmers?

Basic access can remain affordable while revenue comes from completed transaction commissions, optional professional subscriptions, promoted listings, expert services, and institutional capabilities. Charges should always be visible before commitment.

## Closing statement

MlimiConnect connects the pieces that agricultural communities currently manage separately: finding a market, agreeing on value, communicating, fulfilling an order, accessing advice, and proving impact. With the right farmer organisations, buyers, payment providers, logistics partners, and institutions, it can become trusted digital infrastructure for more inclusive agricultural trade in Malawi.
